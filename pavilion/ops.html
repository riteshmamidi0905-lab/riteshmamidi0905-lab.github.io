Model assumptions · Synthetic Austin brewpub

Guests and service

Monday through Sunday, mature demand is 70 / 82 / 96 / 112 / 155 / 175 / 120 covers. The opening ramp is 60% / 74% / 88% / 100% over four weeks. A fixed seed varies each day by −4% to +4%. Each price point above baseline lowers forecast covers by 0.45% of original demand.

One cover calls for one entrée. Each staffed position supports 30 covers per day. An entrée served is the model’s realized-cover proxy. Drinks can sell separately when the kitchen runs short.

Lost guests. A missed meal can cost a return visit. We assume 50% of unserved dining covers forgo one later visit. Of those visits, 50% fall on the next day, 30% on the second and 20% on the third. We round each cohort to whole people and cap the reduction at that day’s expected covers. Drinks sold separately do not count as a served meal. Each forecast links back to the fulfillment checks that reduced it.

Operating costs

4 rotating shift positions, 9 paid hours per position per service. Shift lead $27.00/h · Kitchen lead $24.00/h · Brewhouse $22.00/h · Dining room $19.00/h.

Daily rent $440.00. Utilities $74.00 plus $0.12 per item. Sales tax is 8.25% and settles weekly with operating bills.

Staffing and service over four weeks

The standard brewpub opens with 4 rotating positions. The sweep holds the seed, menu, price, approval limit and delivery time fixed. Lower coverage saves wages, but a longer peak queue costs meals today and visits later. Orders above the $920.00 automatic limit enter the owner queue. Default replays record a simulated owner decision. The weekly budget equals forecast menu units at recipe cost plus 18% for ordering variation. It uses the opening ramp and price-adjusted weekday forecast before any staffing limits. A scenario can set a tighter weekly ceiling. Approved orders reserve budget immediately, including undelivered stock. The remaining budget and uncommitted cash must both cover the order. In a visitor workspace, queued orders wait for an explicit decision.

We assume 60% arrive during a 180-minute peak, with 10 position-minutes per cover. The usual wait is 4 minutes. Excess arrivals wait half the time needed to clear the peak overflow. Beyond 10 minutes, 2% of peak arrivals walk out per extra minute, up to 60%. Each extra minute also disappoints 2% of peak diners who stay, capped at 100%.

Each weekly close rates service from 1 to 5 using the share of arriving diners who got a meal and a satisfactory wait. Half of expected guests are new prospects. Each missing star reduces that group by 12% the following week. Lost return visits apply only to the other half. These rates are synthetic assumptions.
