      Model assumptions · synthetic

      8,200 seats. Eight home games and one concert in 28 days. Normal home demand is 5,850 fans and concert demand 7,580, with deterministic attendance variation. The day 20 featured game forecasts 6,308 fans. A sellout adds 30% up to capacity. A rain-out cancels day 13 and retains one quarter of forecast event labor.

      Per-capita concession demand equals orders per 1,000 fans times the item price divided by 1,000. Gates, pre-start, halftime and close receive 18%, 32%, 37% and 13% of orders. Intervals last 38, 32, 24 and 30 minutes. Twenty percent of unmet demand waits through one more interval. The final queue walks away. Each position has two hours of synthetic pre-event prep at its listed unit rate. Only prepared stock can be handed to fans. Stock is purchased to 132% of forecast demand less inventory. A delayed day 20 shipment reaches stores the following day.

      Each open booth pays the venue 12% of sales plus its event fee. Labor is 5.5 hours per position per event and stand overhead is $15 per day. A halftime shortage removes one worker for 24 minutes. The price-cap scenario limits items to $7. The operator retains a $2.25 home-game or $3.40 concert admission fee per attendee. Starting cash is $120,000 at the venue and $15,000 per vendor.
